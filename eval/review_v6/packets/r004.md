**Title:** Korean BBQ in NYC - A not so definitive ranking . . .

**Post body:**

```text
KBBQ is a hot topic within NYC. With so many options, it’s hard to settle on a consensus, but I think I’ve eaten at enough spots by now to give my not so definitive list. Note that I’ve not been to any of the options in Queens and plan on going to Gaonuri & Bom in the city later this year. Also open to recs.

\- HOWOO - Possibly my favorite high end spot due to the ease of reservation, meat quality, and a robust selection of leafy greens that come with everything from endive to perilla leaves. The value is there despite the higher price tag. The beautiful dining room makes it a great choice for occasions.

\- Yoon Haeundae Galbi - Possibly the best value KBBQ in Manhattan. Excellent cuts of beef & a good selection of greens at very agreeable prices. There was a time when they slimmed down their menu because of sourcing issues, but it seems like they’ve rectified the situation. Also, they do have a tendency to rush tables, so don’t get too comfortable. 

\- Nubiani - Similar to HOWOO, albeit a bit more casual. I think I generally had a better time at HOWOO, but it’s a better fit for a celebration. For regular outings, I’d probably do Nubiani. Their small selection of leafy greens also brings it down a notch for me.

\- Aghassi Gochang - I know about Gopchang Story, but I haven’t been, so this will be my Gopchang restaurant of choice. What you get is a very good selection of Gopchang sets as well as some more agreeable offal-less sets for the less adventurous. The dining room is bright and spacious; a nice change from the usual ambience. Like Nubiani, their leafy greens are a bit lacking. 

\- COTE - Used to be at the top of my list, but it’s lost some luster. I still love the ambience, but the Butcher’s fest seems a bit lacking now that we have so many high end options for KBBQ. It’s not like I weighed the meat, but I feel like I get way more for my money at HOWOO. My last visit actually left me feeling a little hungry. Strange.

\- Jongro/Jongro Gopchang - The quintessential KBBQ in NYC. Yes, you can argue that it’s kinda middle of the road nowadays, but it’s also the baseline for good KBBQ. Their Gopchang space has pretty much the same offerings but with gopchang. I prefer Aghassi over it, but there’s this one caramelized beef intestine dish they make that keeps me coming back. Not sure what it’s called exactly, but it comes with one of the sets. 

\- Jeong Yuk Jeom - One of the newer high end options in the city. I can’t quite put my finger on it, but something was kinda lacking about this place. I think the service was just a tad too casual for what’s seemingly high end spot. It’s pretty much HOWOO, but slightly worse in every way. 

\- ATTI - They wanted to emulate the COTE experience but it’s not quite there. Still very good bbq, but I feel like the lack of customers has lead to a decline in overall quality over the years. It’s in a pretty bad location for a restaurant. 

\- Antoya - A surprisingly solid spot on the Main Street of Koreatown. For the price, there are better options, but if the wait is too long elsewhere, Antoya is an adequate substitute. 

\- Don’s Bogam - A shell of its former self. It’s used to be a very solid spot that had a huge selection of leafy greens to accompany the meat. To this day it was the biggest variety of greens I’ve seen in KBBQ. Sadly, the pandemic happened and they’ve never been the same. The BBQ is just alright for the prices you pay.

\- Let’s Meat - Very middle of the road; nothing offensive and nothing stood out. A great AYCE spot for hungry students who want to stuff their faces on a budget, but unfortunately, I no longer have the stomach for it. 

\- Insa - I started this list and I knew I would put this place on the bottom. Now, I’m not a purist, but this was the only time I went to a KBBQ place and felt like something was wrong. Something about the atmosphere and the banchan selection gave it away. Like the tables were way too far apart. The dining room was weirdly cold and lacking in that communal vibe. I asked my waitress if this place was owned by a white guy and she reluctantly said yes. Listen, I’m not even someone who needs a cuisine to be authentic. When it comes to overrepresented cuisines, I actually prefer they branch out and try something new. Recontexualize it. Get creative. But when you take aspects of a cuisine away, you need to replace it with something else. In Insa’s case, they didn’t replace it with anything. It was KBBQ minus most of the things that made it special. With that said, our server was great. lol 
```

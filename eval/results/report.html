What to do

  1. Re-extract what's stored. 88% of extracted comments went through the original thread prompt, which drops about half the comments in a thread. Re-running them is the largest single improvement to the board.
  2. Use GPT-6 Luna with rubric_v1. It drops 3% of comments one at a time and 1% in threads, and catches 77% of “skip the X” complaints in threads. Thread mode costs about $12 per 100,000 comments.
  3. Cheapest acceptable option: keep DeepSeek v4-flash and switch the backlog to the Oct 2 thread prompt, about $6 per 100,000 comments. It loses dish-level complaints and catches 79% of negatives one comment at a time.
  4. Check Luna's extra mentions. Its precision is 89% against 96% for DeepSeek with the Oct 2 prompt; spot-check before trusting raw mention counts.
  5. Give backfills their own OpenRouter key with its own limit, so a re-extraction can never block the live worker.

How this was measured

  • 769 comments, 825 mentions, labeled blind from the full thread on a 7-level scale, then checked against today's output (23 label corrections).
  • 75% chosen for risk (nameless replies, avoid-lists, strong negatives, long lists, deep chains), 25% at random; single-comment scores are weighted back to real traffic.
  • Every configuration gets the exact input production sends. Comparisons use only comments every row finished.
  • Sonnet 5.5 caught 92% of negatives on the 112 comments it finished. At about $888 per 100,000 comments it shows the ceiling rather than a candidate.
  • Model calls cost $9.72. The run shared production's OpenRouter key and exhausted its limit on Oct 4, which paused live extraction until credit was added.
  • Everything is in eval/ on branch eval-extraction; rerun with run.py, compare.py, report.py.
